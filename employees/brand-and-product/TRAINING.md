# Training the Brand & Product Builder

You have installed them. This is how you get from expertise to a product people
can buy without you in the room.

---

## The trap to avoid first

**Building for months, launching to nobody.**

This is the defining failure of this employee, and it is almost universal.
Building is absorbing, measurable and entirely within your control. Launching is
uncomfortable and depends on other people. So founders build, and build, and
then release something good to an audience that had no idea it was coming, on a
day chosen because that happened to be when the work finished.

The fix is structural, not motivational: **decide the launch date and the
audience before you build**, and start talking about it while you are still
building. The launch skill in this pack is not the last step. Run it early
enough that it can change what you build.

## Day 0 — set up your Business Brain

Before you brief this employee about anything, do the one interview that means
you never explain your business twice:

> Set up my business brain.

The `business-brain` skill asks about your business — who you serve, what you
sell, how you sound, what you will not do, what you are working toward — one
question at a time, about fifteen minutes for the essentials, and writes the
answers to a file called `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md`. Every skill in every pack reads
that file at the start of its own onboarding and asks only for what it does not
answer. Skip Day 0 and each skill interviews you afresh; do it and the interview
happens once.

Where the file lives depends on how you run Claude — the skill tells you, and
`INSTALL.md` says it too. Keep it; it comes with you if you upgrade.

## Before your first session, have these ready

- The expertise you are packaging, and the result you have actually produced
  with it for someone else.
- Who it is for, specifically.
- Whether you have an audience yet, honestly. Zero is a valid answer and changes
  the entire plan.
- How long you will give this before you expect a sale.
- Whether this is a lead magnet or something people pay for. Different products.

## Pick the right format

Do not pick by what appeals. Pick by what the transformation needs.

| If the outcome needs | Build |
|---|---|
| Practice, feedback, doing the thing | **A course** |
| Understanding, credibility, a way in | **A book** |
| Something to happen repeatedly and automatically | **A tool** |
| Your existing service to stop being bespoke | **A productised offer** (Strategist) |

A book is the fastest credibility and the slowest revenue. A course is the
opposite. A tool is the highest ceiling and the highest chance of never
shipping. Choose deliberately.

## Your first few weeks

**Week 1 — Identity, if you do not have one.**

> Build my brand identity.

Personality, naming, colour, typography, a logo brief. Do this before the
product has a face, not after — retrofitting is expensive and the product looks
provisional in the meantime.

If you sell a physical product, this is also where imagery gets solved:

> Build me a shot list and image prompts for my product.

A founder-made product that looks founder-made gets priced like one.

**Week 2 — Plan the launch. Before building.**

> Plan the launch for a course I have not built yet, launching in eight weeks to
> an email list of 200.

Yes, with the real number, even if the real number is zero. A launch plan for an
audience of 200 is a different plan from one for 5,000, and finding that out now
is the entire point.

**Weeks 3-6 — Build.**

> Design the curriculum and learning path for my course.

Then work through it module by module. Do not build all the content before
testing the structure on one real person.

For a book:

> Help me structure my book and write the first chapter.

For software, the coding assistant is written for people who are not developers
and do not intend to become one:

> I want to build a simple tool that does X. Walk me through it.

**Week 7-8 — Pre-launch content, then launch.**

Run the launch plan you made in week 2. This is where the plan earns its keep.

## How to brief well

**Bring real results, not claims.** "I helped a client go from X to Y in Z
weeks, here is roughly how" produces a curriculum grounded in something. "I am
good at marketing" produces a generic one.

**Say who this is *not* for.** Sharpens everything downstream, especially the
launch messaging.

**Test the structure before writing the content.** Show the curriculum to one
person in your audience and ask where they would get stuck. Bring their answer
back:

> They said module 3 assumes they already have a list. Restructure for that.

**For software, say your actual skill level.** It calibrates. Overstating it
gets you instructions you cannot follow; understating gets you a tour of things
you already know.

---

## Mistakes that cost people the most

**Building before planning the launch.** Named twice. It is the one.

**Making it too big.** The instinct is to over-deliver — twelve modules where
four would do. A course people finish sells the next thing. A course people
abandon does not, however comprehensive it was.

**Perfecting the logo while the product does not exist.** Identity is week one
and it is time-boxed. It is the most comfortable work here and the least
load-bearing.

**Launching to nobody and concluding the product was bad.** Almost always a
distribution failure wearing a product failure's clothes. If your audience is
small, the Content Marketer is the prerequisite, not an afterthought.

## The ceiling of this employee, and the way past it

With a Business Brain, the product, the audience and the brand are no longer
re-explained every session — `build-your-own-brand-identity` hands the visual
identity back into the file, and every later skill reads it. What still costs is
the build itself: by module nine of a course, re-establishing *where you got to*
is most of the session, because it cannot see your drafts, your files or your
progress, and it never sees a student.

For getting a first product built and out, that is a fair trade and this pack is
genuinely enough. The ceiling is specific: it arrives when the project is large
enough that carrying its context yourself becomes the work.

That is what the Client Operating System is for — the same skills, with the build
carrying its own context between sessions. **Your `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md` comes with
you:** paste it on the Business Brain page, or tell your own Claude to *import my
business brain from BUSINESS-BRAIN.md*, and the product, the brand and the
audience are already known.
