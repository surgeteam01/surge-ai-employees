# The Brand & Product Builder

> Builds the thing you sell, and makes it look like it is worth the price.

**Hire them when:** I have expertise people pay me for one hour at a time, and I want to turn it into something that sells without me in the room.

---

## What they do

Every service business eventually hits the same ceiling: revenue is bounded by
hours, and hours do not scale. The way through is a product — something that
carries your expertise without carrying your calendar. A course, a book, a tool,
a productised offer.

The Brand & Product Builder does both halves of that. It builds the asset —
curriculum through to launch for a course, premise through to finished
manuscript for a book, idea through to deployed software for a tool, with the
coding assistant written for someone who is not a developer and does not intend
to become one. And it builds the *presentation*, which is not decoration: a
visual identity, and product imagery that does not look like it was taken on a
kitchen table, because a founder-made product that looks founder-made gets
priced like one.

Then it launches it, which is a distinct skill from building it and the one
people most often discover they needed on the day they had planned to launch.

Take the launch seriously in particular. The most common failure here is not a
bad product. It is a good product released to an audience that had no idea it
was coming, on a day chosen because that happened to be when the work finished.

## The two that come with every employee

**The AI COO** is the front door. It knows all four employees and every skill
each one holds -- and this copy was built for *this* pack, so it knows which
skills are installed here and which live in an employee you have not hired. Ask
it for something outside this pack and it names the employee that has it,
rather than improvising a worse answer from the wrong tool.

**The Business Brain** is the memory. One interview, written to a file every
other skill reads first, so nobody explains their business twice. Start there.
