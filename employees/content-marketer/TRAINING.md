# Training the Content Marketer

You have installed them. This is how you get content out of them that sounds
like you and that people actually read.

---

## Do this before anything else

**Capture your voice. It takes twenty minutes and it changes every other output
in this pack.**

> Here are five things I have written. Build my voice profile.

Paste real writing — old emails to clients, a LinkedIn post that did well, a
message to a friend explaining what you do, anything genuinely yours. Rough is
better than polished. Voice notes transcribed are excellent, because that is
actually how you talk.

Skip this step and everything downstream is competent, publishable, and
unmistakably machine-written. Em-dashes everywhere, "in today's fast-paced
world", relentless three-item lists, an enthusiasm you do not have. Readers
cannot always name why it feels off, but they can feel it, and they do not
follow it.

Do it once. Then reference it every time:

> Write these using my voice profile.

---

## Pick one channel. Seriously.

This employee can run LinkedIn, YouTube, a faceless channel, a podcast and SEO.
You can run one, maybe two. A founder posting well on one channel for six months
beats a founder posting badly on four for six weeks, every single time.

Choose by what you can sustain, not by where the audience is:

| If you | Choose |
|---|---|
| Write more easily than you talk | **LinkedIn** |
| Talk more easily than you write | **Podcast** or **YouTube** |
| Will not go on camera | **Faceless channel** |
| Want traffic that compounds while you sleep | **SEO** |
| Have no idea | **LinkedIn** — lowest production cost, fastest feedback |

Commit for ninety days before you judge it.

## Day 0 — set up your Business Brain

Before you brief this employee about anything, do the one interview that means
you never explain your business twice:

> Set up my business brain.

The `business-brain` skill asks about your business — who you serve, what you
sell, how you sound, what you will not do, what you are working toward — one
question at a time, about fifteen minutes for the essentials, and writes the
answers to a file called `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md`. Every skill in every pack reads
that file at the start of its own onboarding and asks only for what it does not
answer. Skip Day 0 and each skill interviews you afresh; do it and the interview
happens once.

Where the file lives depends on how you run Claude — the skill tells you, and
`INSTALL.md` says it too. Keep it; it comes with you if you upgrade.

---

## Before your first session, have these ready

- What you sell and who it is for. If you cannot say this in a sentence, run the
  Business Strategist first — content aimed at everyone reaches no one.
- Five pieces of your own writing, for the voice profile.
- How many hours a week you will genuinely give this. Be honest and be pessimistic.
- Three things you believe about your industry that others in it would dispute.

That last one is where good content comes from. Agreeable content is invisible.

## Your first week

**Day 1 — Voice profile.** As above. Do not skip it.

**Day 2 — The calendar.**

> Build me a 30-day content calendar for LinkedIn.

You get themes, a cadence, and thirty specific pieces with hooks — planned
around what you actually sell, so the content has somewhere to lead.

**Day 3 — Write the first week, then rewrite it yourself.**

> Write days 1 to 7 in my voice.

Then edit them by hand. Every one. This is not wasted effort — you are training
your own eye for where it drifts off-voice, and you will need that eye for the
next twenty-three days when you are moving faster.

**Day 4 — Post one. Not a good one. Any one.**

The founders who succeed with this are not the ones with the best calendar. They
are the ones who published on day four.

**Day 5 — Set up your channel properly.**

> Optimise my LinkedIn profile and headline.

Traffic to a weak profile converts to nothing. Do this once, early.

## How to brief well

**Give it the actual story.** "Write a post about client onboarding" produces a
listicle. "A client ghosted me for three weeks and then said they had been too
embarrassed to admit they had not done the homework — write about that"
produces something people comment on. Specifics are the raw material.

**Always name the voice profile.** Every session. It does not carry over.

**Tell it what did well.** Paste your top-performing post and say *more like
this, here are the numbers*. It will find the pattern.

**Ask for angles before drafts.**

> Give me ten angles on this before writing anything.

Pick two. You will get better output than accepting the first draft of the first
angle, and it costs you thirty seconds.

---

## Mistakes that cost people the most

**Skipping the voice profile.** Named twice deliberately. It is the difference
between content and noise.

**Batching thirty posts, publishing four.** Write a week, publish a week, then
write the next. Momentum beats inventory.

**Posting and vanishing.** Especially on LinkedIn — replying to comments for
twenty minutes after posting does more for reach than the post did.

**Changing channel at week three.** Three weeks is not a result. It is a warm-up.

**Content with nowhere to go.** Every piece should have a next step, even a soft
one. Content that builds an audience with no path to an offer builds an
audience, not a business.

## When it says "that is not my job"

**The AI COO** is in this pack, and it is the front door. It knows all four
employees and every skill each one holds — and this copy was built for *this*
pack, so it knows what is installed here. Ask it to write your sales page and it
tells you plainly that the sales page, the email funnel and the ads live with the
Growth & Sales Rep, and that positioning and pricing live with the Business
Strategist — and it stops there rather than improvising a worse answer.

That is the honest answer, and it is also the map.

## The ceiling of this employee, and the way past it

With a Business Brain, your voice profile is no longer something you re-paste
every session — `write-like-a-human` hands it back into the file, and every
later skill reads it. What they still cannot do is see your analytics, post for
you, schedule anything, or check whether last month's calendar actually ran.
"What worked" is whatever you remember to tell them.

For getting from *not posting* to *posting consistently*, that is a fair trade
and this pack is genuinely enough. You will feel the ceiling when you want next
month's calendar built from what actually performed rather than from your
impression of it.

That is the Client Operating System — same skills, your channels connected,
performance feeding back into the next plan. **Your `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md` comes
with you:** paste it on the Business Brain page, or tell your own Claude to
*import my business brain from BUSINESS-BRAIN.md*, and your voice and your
business are already loaded.
