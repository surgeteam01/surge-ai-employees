# Training the Growth & Sales Rep

You have installed them. This is how you get a system that actually books calls,
rather than assets that sit in a folder.

---

## Read this before you brief them once

**This employee amplifies whatever you give it, including a bad offer.**

Every other guide in this pack can survive a shaky foundation. This one cannot.
A sales page for an offer nobody wants converts at zero, and it will look like a
copy problem, so you will rewrite the copy, and it will still convert at zero.
Cold outreach to a vague ICP produces polite silence at scale.

If you cannot answer *who this is for* and *why they would pay this much*, run
the Business Strategist first. One day there saves a month here.

## Day 0 — set up your Business Brain

Before you brief this employee about anything, do the one interview that means
you never explain your business twice:

> Set up my business brain.

The `business-brain` skill asks about your business — who you serve, what you
sell, how you sound, what you will not do, what you are working toward — one
question at a time, about fifteen minutes for the essentials, and writes the
answers to a file called `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md`. Every skill in every pack reads
that file at the start of its own onboarding and asks only for what it does not
answer. Skip Day 0 and each skill interviews you afresh; do it and the interview
happens once.

Where the file lives depends on how you run Claude — the skill tells you, and
`INSTALL.md` says it too. Keep it; it comes with you if you upgrade.

## Before your first session, have these ready

- Your offer, and its price.
- Who buys it — the narrower the better. "Ecommerce founders" is not an ICP.
  "Shopify stores doing £50k–£200k a month who have just hired their first
  marketer" is.
- Where your last five clients came from.
- Your actual conversion numbers, if you have them. Rough is fine. None is fine
  too, but say so rather than guessing.
- What you will not do. No cold calling, no discounting, no ads budget — say it
  up front so the system it designs is one you will actually run.

## Build in this order

The order matters more here than anywhere else in this pack. Each step feeds the
next, and skipping ahead means redoing work.

**1. The sales page — even if you never publish it.**

> Build me a sales page for my offer.

Do this first regardless of channel. It forces every argument for your offer
into the open: the promise, the proof, the objections in the order a real buyer
has them, the guarantee, the price justification. Every ad, email and outreach
message downstream is a compressed version of an argument you make here.

Founders who skip this write ads that sell nothing, because they never worked
out what the sale actually is.

**2. Capture — give them a reason to raise a hand.**

> Design a lead capture system for my business.

A mechanism worth an email address, scoring so you spend your hours on people
who might buy, and follow-up that happens whether or not you remember.

**3. Follow-up — where most of the money is.**

> Build my email funnel: welcome, nurture, and a sales sequence.

Most founders lose more revenue to silence after interest than to a lack of
interest. This step is unglamorous and it is usually the highest-return thing in
this guide.

**4. Then, and only then, a traffic source.**

Cold outreach if you know exactly who to target and have no budget. Ads if you
have budget and something proven to point it at. A webinar if your offer needs
explaining before anyone will buy.

> Build me a cold outreach sequence for [specific ICP].

Never point paid traffic at an unproven funnel. That is how you buy the
knowledge that your funnel is broken, at the worst possible price.

## How to brief well

**Be specific about the ICP to the point of discomfort.** Job title, company
size, the trigger event that makes them need you this month. Personalisation
research is what separates outreach that gets replies from outreach that gets
reported.

**Give it real objections.** The actual sentences prospects say to you. "It's
too expensive", "we tried something like this before", "I need to ask my
partner". Real objections in, real rebuttals out.

**Tell it what has already failed.** "I sent 200 cold emails and got two
replies, here is the email." It will diagnose the email rather than write you
the same one again.

**Ask for the numbers behind the design.**

> What conversion rate does this funnel assume at each step, and what happens to
> the model if the first step is half that?

If it cannot answer, the design is decorative. Push until it can.

## Mistakes that cost people the most

**Building the machine before validating the offer.** Named twice. It is the one
that wastes months.

**Automating outreach before a message has worked manually.** Send fifty by
hand. When one version gets replies, automate that. Automating an untested
message just fails faster and burns the domain doing it.

**Ignoring deliverability.** The best cold email in the world in a spam folder
is nothing. Ask directly: *what do I need to set up before sending?* Do that
first.

**Writing follow-ups that only say "just checking in".** Every follow-up should
carry something new. The sequence it builds will do this; do not water it down.

**Treating the webinar as the product.** The webinar sells the product. If the
offer at the end is vague, a great webinar produces great feedback and no sales.

## The ceiling of this employee, and the way past it

It designs the system. It does not run it. Nothing here sends an email, watches
a reply, updates a CRM record or notices that a sequence has stopped converting.
You are the runtime. With a Business Brain the offer, the ICP and the numbers are
no longer re-explained each session — but the operating still is not done for you.

For designing a funnel you did not have, that is a fair trade and this pack is
genuinely enough. The ceiling arrives sharply and you will know it: the day the
design is done and the actual bottleneck becomes *operating* it — chasing
replies, keeping the CRM honest, remembering who to follow up on Thursday.

That is what the Client Operating System is for — the same skills, connected to
your CRM and inbox, with the follow-up actually happening rather than being
described. **Your `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md` comes with you:** paste it on the Business
Brain page, or tell your own Claude to *import my business brain from
BUSINESS-BRAIN.md*, and the design you built here is the one it runs.
