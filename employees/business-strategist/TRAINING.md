# Training the Business Strategist

You have installed them. This is how you get work out of them that is worth
acting on.

---

## The one idea that changes everything

**Brief them like a consultant, not like a search engine.**

A search engine gets a keyword. A consultant you have just hired gets context —
what the business does, what you have tried, what is actually worrying you at
2am. The difference in output is not small. It is the difference between generic
advice you could have found in a blog post and advice that is about *your*
business.

Every skill in this pack opens with an onboarding stage designed to interview
you. **Let it.** The single most common way founders waste this employee is
answering the opening questions in five words each because they want to get to
the deliverable. The deliverable is made *of* those answers.

## Day 0 — set up your Business Brain

Before you brief this employee about anything, do the one interview that means
you never explain your business twice:

> Set up my business brain.

The `business-brain` skill asks about your business — who you serve, what you
sell, how you sound, what you will not do, what you are working toward — one
question at a time, about fifteen minutes for the essentials, and writes the
answers to a file called `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md`. Every skill in every pack reads
that file at the start of its own onboarding and asks only for what it does not
answer. Skip Day 0 and each skill interviews you afresh; do it and the interview
happens once.

Where the file lives depends on how you run Claude — the skill tells you, and
`INSTALL.md` says it too. Keep it; it comes with you if you upgrade.

## Before your first session, have these ready

You do not need documents. You need to be able to answer, out loud:

- What you sell, and to whom.
- Roughly what you charged last month, and roughly what it cost you to deliver.
- Where your last five clients came from.
- What you would fix first if you had a free week.
- What you are afraid the answer is.

That last one matters more than it looks. An audit you have already
pre-emptively defended against is an audit that tells you what you wanted to
hear.

## Your first week

**Day 1 — Run the audit.**

> Run a full audit of my business.

It will interview you, then work across offer, positioning, marketing, funnel,
pricing, margins, operations, capacity, retention and risk. It ends with a
ranked opportunity matrix — every finding scored by impact and effort, each one
mapped to the specific thing that fixes it.

Do not skip ahead when it asks about an area you think is fine. The areas
founders are confident about are where the expensive surprises live.

**Day 2 — Sit with it. Do not act yet.**

Read the matrix. Argue with it. Push back in the conversation:

> The pricing finding assumes I can raise prices without losing my two biggest
> clients. Both are on annual contracts. Redo that finding with that constraint.

It will revise. A finding that survives your pushback is one you can act on; a
finding that collapses was based on something it assumed and you never told it.

**Day 3 — Fix the foundation, if it is broken.**

If the audit flagged positioning:

> Help me find my niche.

If it flagged pricing or margins:

> Rebuild my pricing.

If it flagged that every job is quoted from scratch:

> Turn my service into a productised offer.

Run these in that order if more than one was flagged. Pricing set before the
niche is clear gets redone.

**Day 4-5 — Take one real question to the analyst.**

Different job from the audit. The audit is qualitative and broad; the analyst
takes data you actually have and answers one specific question rigorously.

> Here is my last 12 months of invoices as a CSV. Which client segment is
> actually profitable once I account for delivery hours?

Paste real numbers. This is the skill that most rewards real data and most
punishes vagueness.

## How to brief well

**Give the honest version.** "Revenue is fine but I hate the work" is a real
strategic input and it will change the recommendation. Founders routinely hide
this and then wonder why the plan is one they do not want to execute.

**Name your constraints up front.** Hours per week. Cash available. Things you
will not do — no cold calling, no video on camera, no discounting. A plan that
ignores a real constraint is not a plan.

**Ask for the reasoning, then check it.**

> Why is that ranked above the pricing fix?

If the reasoning is weak, say so. It will revise. This employee is at its best
in argument, not dictation.

**Correct it when it is wrong about you.** It is working from what you told it.
If a finding rests on a wrong assumption, name the assumption rather than
rejecting the finding — you will get a corrected finding instead of a defensive
one.

## Mistakes that cost people the most

**Running the audit, agreeing with it, and doing nothing.** By far the most
common. The matrix is ranked so you can pick one thing. Pick one thing.

**Fixing what is easy instead of what is first.** The top of the matrix is
usually harder than item four. Item four is not a substitute.

**Treating the output as final.** It is a first draft by someone who has known
your business for twenty minutes. It is meant to be argued with.

**Using `master-ai-prompting` last.** Use it early. It teaches you to brief
every other employee in this pack better, and it pays for itself immediately.

## When it says "that is not my job"

**The AI COO** is in this pack, and it is the front door. It knows all four
employees and every skill each one holds — and this copy was built for *this*
pack, so it knows which skills are installed here and which are not. Ask it for
something outside the Strategist and it names the employee that has it — the
Growth & Sales Rep for a sales page or lead flow, the Content Marketer for a
calendar — and stops there rather than improvising a worse answer.

That is working as intended, not a gap. The honest answer and the map are the
same sentence.

## The ceiling of this employee, and the way past it

Worth knowing, so you recognise it when you hit it.

They cannot see your CRM, your bank feed or your calendar — you paste in what
they need, and their findings are as good as what you remembered to mention. With
a Business Brain they no longer start every conversation knowing nothing, but
what they produce is still not retained between sessions unless you keep it.

For a founder finding their footing, that is a fair trade and this pack is
genuinely enough. The point at which it stops being enough is specific: when you
want the audit run against your actual numbers rather than your recollection of
them, and when carrying context yourself becomes the work.

That is what the Client Operating System is for — the same skills, your systems
connected, and the work running without you selecting the tool. **Your
`BUSINESS-BRAIN.md` comes with you:** paste it on the Business Brain page, or tell
your own Claude to *import my business brain from BUSINESS-BRAIN.md*, and nothing
you told these employees is typed again.
