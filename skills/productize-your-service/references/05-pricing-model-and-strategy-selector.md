# Module 5: Pricing Model & Strategy Selector

**Purpose:** Choose the pricing model and market positioning strategy for the productized package before setting actual numbers — reused directly from `nail-your-pricing`, since the underlying decision (how the price is structured, and where it sits relative to alternatives) is identical regardless of whether the offer is a digital product or a service package.

## Process

1. **Choose a pricing model** based on the scope defined in Module 4 and the delivery pattern: **flat fee per engagement** (best for a discrete, one-time deliverable — a website audit, a brand identity package), **retainer/subscription** (best when the service repeats on a cadence — monthly bookkeeping, ongoing content production), or **tiered flat fee** (multiple fixed packages at different scope levels — the most common productized-service pattern). Usage-based and value-based models are rarer fits for productized services but flag them if the offer's delivery pattern genuinely matches (e.g. a done-for-you ads management fee tied to ad spend managed).
2. **Choose a positioning strategy**: **premium** (priced above typical freelance/agency rates in the space, justified by the predictability and quality productization enables — requires strong proof), **mid-market/competitive** (priced near comparable service providers, differentiated on the productized experience itself), or **value/penetration** (priced to win volume while the business builds a portfolio of productized case studies — only recommend this if margin supports it, and flag the risk of being stuck at a low price once demand exists).
3. **Flag fear-based pricing** directly, the same as in `nail-your-pricing` — a common trap in productizing a service is under-pricing the package because it feels "too simple" compared to the bespoke version, even though the value delivered to the client hasn't shrunk, only the delivery friction has.

## Output format

```
PRICING MODEL: [flat fee / retainer / tiered flat fee / other] — reason: [scope + delivery pattern]
POSITIONING STRATEGY: [premium / mid-market / value] — reason: [market + differentiation + margin reality]
FEAR-PRICING FLAG: [if applicable — is the price being pulled down because the service now looks "simpler," despite delivering the same value]
```

## Handoff

The model and positioning chosen here shape Module 6 (tier design must fit the model) and Module 7 (the price point calculation happens within this strategy).
