# Module 1: The interview

**Purpose:** Find the job. People do not think in capabilities; nobody wakes up wanting
`crm.list_leads`. They wake up wanting someone to notice when a lead goes quiet. The
interview turns the second sentence into the first.

## The rules

- **One question at a time.** Never a numbered list of questions. Wait for the answer,
  then ask the next one.
- **Read the brain first.** The Business Brain already says who the business is, who it
  serves, what it sells and how it sounds. Ask nothing it answers.
- **Their words, not yours.** Write down what they said. "Leads that nobody replied to"
  is the outcome; "lead-nurture automation" is your paraphrase, and it goes nowhere.
- **Stop when you have enough.** You need the role, three outcomes, what "good" looks
  like, what it must never do, and how often it runs. Five to eight questions; never
  more than twelve.

## The questions, in order

Skip any the brain or the request already answers.

1. **What keeps falling through the cracks?** The work nobody owns. Let them describe
   it before naming it.
2. **If somebody owned that, what would be true every week?** Push for results, not
   activities: "every quiet lead gets a follow-up" rather than "checks the CRM". You
   need **three** of these; seven is the most a design holds.
3. **Who is it for?** Them, a colleague, a client, a department. This decides who reads
   the output and where it goes.
4. **Show me a good one.** A real example — a message that would have been right, a
   report they liked. This becomes an **evaluation case**: the situation, the request
   they would make, and what a good answer contains. Ask for two more over the
   interview; a design carries at least three.
5. **What must it never do?** Send something, spend money, change a record, talk to a
   client. Each "never" becomes the approval floor the design asks for, and a check in
   a case.
6. **How often, and what starts it?** Every Monday, when a form arrives, when they ask.
   This becomes the routine the design proposes, or "on request".
7. **What do you already use for this?** Tools, apps, a spreadsheet, a person. This is
   the first list you check in Module 2; it is not the inventory.
8. **What would you pay per run, and how fast must it be?** Optional. It shapes the tier
   recommendation in Module 4: a look-up, a quick answer, or real work.

## What you write down

```
role:      the job as a title, e.g. "The Quiet-Lead Chaser"
hiresFor:  the problem in their words, one or two sentences
outcomes:  three to seven results, each one line
good:      two or three examples that become cases (Module 4)
nevers:    what it must never do
cadence:   when it runs, and what starts it
uses:      what they already use for this
```

## Handoff

To Module 2 with the list above. Do not design yet: a design made before the
inventory is read names tools that do not exist.
