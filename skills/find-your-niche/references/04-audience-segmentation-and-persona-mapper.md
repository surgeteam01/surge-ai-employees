# Module 4: Audience Segmentation & Persona Mapper

**Purpose:** Break "everyone" or a vague broad audience into 3-5 distinct, real candidate segments — this is the point where the pipeline stops working with an undifferentiated mass and starts working with specific groups of people who can actually be researched, targeted, and served differently.

## Process

1. **Generate candidate segments** by combining Module 2's skills/experience/advantages with Module 3's identified gaps — each candidate segment should connect to something real the person can deliver, and ideally sit near a gap in the market landscape rather than a crowded axis.
2. **Define each segment concretely**: who they are (role, industry, life stage, or situation — not just demographics), what specific situation makes them different from the broader "everyone" (e.g. not "small business owners" but "solo consultants who just hit their first $10k month and don't have systems in place yet"), and roughly how reachable they are (is there an identifiable place they gather, search, or can be found?).
3. **Generate 3-5 genuinely distinct segments**, not minor variations of the same one — the value of this module is giving Module 6 real alternatives to compare, not a false choice between near-identical options.
4. **Flag segments that are attractive but essentially untested guesses** versus ones grounded in Module 1's "historical requests for help" or Module 2's proven results — this distinction matters for Module 6's confidence scoring.

## Output format

```
CANDIDATE SEGMENTS:

SEGMENT 1: [name/description]
Who specifically: [concrete description, not vague demographics]
Specific situation: [what makes them different from "everyone"]
Reachability: [identifiable channels/communities, or "unclear — needs research"]
Grounded in: [connects to which skill/result/advantage from Module 2, or which gap from Module 3]

(repeat for 3-5 segments total)
```

## Handoff

These candidate segments feed Module 5 (customer research runs per segment) and Module 6 (viability scoring compares them directly against each other). Present all segments to the user before narrowing — the choice of which to pursue further should involve them, not be made unilaterally.
