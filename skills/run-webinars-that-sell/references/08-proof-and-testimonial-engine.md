# Module 8: Proof & Testimonial Engine

**Purpose:** Turn raw results and rough testimonials into persuasive, specific proof blocks matched to specific moments in the webinar — the content sections (building belief throughout) and the pitch (reducing purchase risk right before the ask). Vague praise does almost nothing; specific, outcome-focused proof does the heavy lifting.

## Process

1. **Categorize available proof** into types: results/case studies (numbers, before-after), social proof (volume — "over 1,000 people have gone through this"), authority proof (credentials, media, notable clients), and peer proof (testimonials from people like the attendee).
2. **For each raw testimonial, extract the specific outcome** buried inside it. Rewrite for clarity and impact without inventing anything — never fabricate a result, number, or quote that wasn't given. If a testimonial is vague, either ask the user for the specific result behind it or use it only as supporting color, not as headline proof.
3. **Match proof to moment, not just to a general library**: authority/social proof works well in the open (quick credibility boost); a specific case study or result fits naturally right after the content section it validates; peer testimonials fit best right before or during the pitch, when the attendee is deciding whether this will work for someone like them.
4. **Write proof in a spoken-friendly, slightly longer form** than a page pull-quote would use — live delivery can carry a fuller story than a written testimonial fragment, so include enough narrative detail that it lands when spoken aloud, not just when read.

## Output format

```
PROOF BLOCKS:

[Type: Result/Authority/Social/Peer]
Spoken version: "[2-4 sentence version, written to be said aloud]"
Best placement: [Open / Content Section n / Pitch]

(repeat per proof piece)

GAPS: [what proof is missing that would strengthen the webinar — flag for the user to go collect]
```

## Handoff

If "none yet" was the answer in Module 1, say so plainly here rather than inventing placeholder testimonials — recommend a first proof strategy instead (e.g. a founder's-story-as-proof angle for the open, or leaning harder on the guarantee in Module 11 to substitute for missing social proof). Proof placements noted here fill the markers left in Module 7 and feed directly into Module 11's pitch.
