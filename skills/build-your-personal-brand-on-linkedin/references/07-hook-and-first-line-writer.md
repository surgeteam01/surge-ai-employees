# Module 7: Hook & First-Line Writer

**Purpose:** Write the first 1-2 lines of each post — the single most consequential piece of copy on LinkedIn, since the platform truncates posts behind a "see more" link after roughly the first 2-3 lines, meaning the hook alone decides whether the rest gets read.

## Process

1. **Write the hook to work as a complete, curiosity-generating thought on its own**, visible before any truncation — a hook that trails off mid-sentence right at the truncation point undersells the post; a hook that's already a satisfying, curiosity-inducing statement earns the click to expand.
2. **Draw on Module 3's exact-phrase bank and Module 6's specific idea/story** — a hook grounded in something specific and real ("I got fired from my first sales job. Here's the one thing I wish someone had told me before that happened.") outperforms a generic, broad opener.
3. **Avoid generic LinkedIn-cliché openers** ("I am thrilled to announce," "Big news," used reflexively rather than when genuinely warranted) — these have become so common they now suppress curiosity rather than build it.
4. **Use short lines and line breaks even within the hook** — LinkedIn's format rewards visual scannability from the very first line, not just later in the post.
5. **Vary hook style across the calendar** — story opener, bold contrarian statement, a specific question, a numbers-led statement — so the feed doesn't read as formulaic.

## Output format

```
POST [n]:
HOOK (first 1-2 lines, pre-truncation): [...]
Hook style: [story / contrarian statement / question / numbers-led]
```

## Handoff

Feeds Module 8's full post structure — the hook is the post's first lines, not a separate artifact.
