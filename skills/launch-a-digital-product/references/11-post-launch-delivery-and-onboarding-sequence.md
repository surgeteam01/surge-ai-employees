# Module 11: Post-Launch Delivery & Onboarding Sequence

**Purpose:** Design the buyer's very first experience after purchasing — this is the single biggest lever on refund rate and product completion, and it's the stage most launches under-invest in relative to the pre-sale copywriting.

## Process

1. **Write the immediate delivery/access message** — sent the moment payment completes: how to access the product (login details, download link, community invite), what to do first, and a warm, confidence-building line reinforcing that they made a good decision (buyer's remorse peaks in the first 24-48 hours — this message is the first defense against it).
2. **Design a short onboarding sequence** (2-4 emails over the first 1-2 weeks) that drives the buyer to their first genuine win inside the product as quickly as possible — the single best predictor of low refunds and high completion is an early quick win, not comprehensive coverage of everything the product contains.
3. **Address the product type's specific onboarding needs** (from Module 1): a course needs a clear "start here" path through the curriculum; software needs a setup/first-use walkthrough; a template/toolkit needs a fast "here's the one thing to do right now" nudge; a community needs an introduction/first-post prompt.
4. **Include a clear support/help path** in the first message — buyers who don't know how to get help when stuck are the ones most likely to quietly disengage and later request a refund.
5. **If a guarantee period applies** (from the sales page's guarantee, Module 6), don't mention the refund window prominently in onboarding — the goal here is engagement and confidence, not reminding buyers how to back out.

## Output format

```
IMMEDIATE DELIVERY MESSAGE
Body: [full message — access details, first step, confidence-building line, support path]

ONBOARDING SEQUENCE ([count] emails/messages):
MESSAGE 1 — [first genuine win] (Timing: [x])
Body: [full message]
MESSAGE 2 — [next step / deeper engagement] (Timing: [x])
Body: [full message]
(repeat per message)
```

## Handoff

This sequence feeds Module 13's assembly at the "post-purchase" trigger from Module 4's timeline. Module 14 should specifically flag if this module was rushed or thin relative to the pre-sale materials, since a launch that invests heavily in the pitch and little in delivery is setting up its own refund/reputation problem.
