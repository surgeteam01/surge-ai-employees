# Module 10: Guarantee Writer

**Purpose:** Write an ethical, confidence-building guarantee that reduces the buyer's perceived risk without over-promising or creating a claim the business can't honor.

## Process

1. **Confirm what the business can actually stand behind** — never write a guarantee stronger than what the user has confirmed they'll honor. If unclear, ask before drafting.
2. **Choose a guarantee type that fits the offer:**
   - **Unconditional** (e.g. "30-day money back, no questions asked") — highest trust, best when confident in product-market fit.
   - **Conditional** (refund if the buyer does X and doesn't get Y) — reduces risk of refund abuse, still builds confidence.
   - **Performance-based** (tied to a specific measurable outcome) — strongest trust signal, only use if the outcome is genuinely reliable and measurable.
   - **Extended/double guarantee** (e.g. "keep the bonuses even if you refund") — useful when the core guarantee alone feels standard.
3. **Write the guarantee in plain, warm language** — avoid legalistic hedging that undercuts the confidence it's meant to convey, while staying accurate to the real terms.
4. **Give it a name.** Block 11 of `00-page-blueprint.md` has a *named* guarantee with a badge, not a paragraph headed "Guarantee" — a name turns a policy into a promise the business is willing to be held to, and it gives the FAQ and the founder letter something to refer back to.
5. **Hit all five required parts.** The blueprint's block 11 is specific about what the copy must contain, in this order:
   - **Unshakeable confidence** in the offer, stated plainly up front.
   - **The length** of the guarantee window.
   - **The specific actions** they will be taking during that window to produce the outcome. This is what makes the guarantee credible rather than a returns policy — it tells the buyer what "gave it a fair go" means before they have to guess.
   - **The specific tangible and emotional benefits** they should see inside that window. Both: a number they can check, and how it will feel.
   - **The exact refund process** — who they contact, how, and what happens next. An undisclosed condition here is the fastest route from customer to complaint, so the process on the page is the real one or the guarantee is rewritten to match reality.

## Output format

```
GUARANTEE NAME: [the named guarantee, e.g. "The 30-Day First-Client Guarantee"]
BADGE SLOT: [what the badge should say]
GUARANTEE TYPE: [chosen type] — reason: [why this fits the offer/business stage]
GUARANTEE COPY:
  Confidence line: [...]
  Length: [...]
  Actions they'll take: [...]
  Benefits they should see (tangible + emotional): [...]
  Exact refund process: [who they contact, how, what happens]
FINE PRINT (if needed): [any necessary conditions, written plainly]
CONFIRMED WITH USER: [yes / no — never ship an unconfirmed guarantee]
```

## Handoff

This feeds Module 11 (it sits directly beside or under the pricing section), and the copy is reused **verbatim** as FAQ slot 5 in Module 9 rather than paraphrased there — two differently-worded versions of the same guarantee on one page reads as small print.
