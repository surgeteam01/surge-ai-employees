# Module 6: Lead Writer

**Purpose:** Write the opening paragraphs that hook the reader immediately after the headline — the "lead" in direct-response terms. This is where readers decide to keep scrolling or leave.

## Process

1. **Pick a lead style that matches awareness stage** (from Module 3):
   - **Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS)** — name the problem, twist the knife on the cost of not solving it, then open the door to a solution. Best for problem-aware/unaware audiences.
   - **Story/scene opener** — drop the reader into a specific, relatable moment (avoid generic "Have you ever felt..." openers; be concrete). Good for building emotional connection before pitching.
   - **Bold claim + proof tease** — state the result up front and tease the proof to come. Best for warmer, more skeptical/product-aware audiences who want to see the goods fast.
   - **Direct address** — speak straight to the reader's specific situation ("If you're a [specific role] who [specific situation], this is for you"). Good for short-form, most-aware pages.
2. **Use the exact-phrase bank** from Module 3 in the opening lines — this is where mirroring the customer's own words matters most, since it's the first proof the page "gets" them.
3. **Write 2-3 variants** in different styles so the user has a real choice, not one option dressed up as a decision.
4. **Then write the blueprint's qualifier-and-head-nod block** (block 2 of `00-page-blueprint.md`). This is the default lead shape for this pack and it is structurally specific, so build it deliberately:

   - **Open with the identity triple** — "If you're a [instant identifier 1], [instant identifier 2], or [instant identifier 3] looking to [tangible outcome] while [emotional or less tangible outcome], then you already know that… [the thing they already know]". Three identifiers, so more than one kind of buyer sees themselves.
   - **"You already know" is doing the work.** Give the reader credit for what they have already figured out instead of explaining it to them. It is the difference between a page that flatters and a page that lectures, and the lecture loses.
   - **Land at least three head-nod moments** — statements this specific buyer would nod along to. Take them verbatim from Module 3's phrase bank; a head-nod written from imagination reads as a guess.
   - **Close by giving permission to continue** — tell them plainly they are in the right place and to keep reading. The block ends with the reader's next action decided.
   - **Carries an image** (block 2 in the blueprint). Note the image slot even if the user has no asset yet.

   A lead variant from step 1–3 can *feed* this block, but it does not replace it — the qualifier is what makes the whole page feel addressed to one person.

## Output format

```
LEAD VARIANT 1 ([style name]):
[2-4 short paragraphs]

LEAD VARIANT 2 ([style name]):
[2-4 short paragraphs]

RECOMMENDATION: [which one and why, tied to awareness stage and voice]

QUALIFIER BLOCK (blueprint block 2):
Identity line: If you're a [id 1], [id 2], or [id 3] looking to [tangible outcome] while [emotional outcome], then you already know that… [...]
Head-nods: 1. [...] 2. [...] 3. [...]
Permission line: [...]
Image slot: [what it should show, or NEEDS INPUT]
```

## Handoff

Whichever lead is chosen sets the tone for the rest of the page — Module 13 (Assembler) should carry its voice and pacing forward rather than letting later sections drift into a different register.
