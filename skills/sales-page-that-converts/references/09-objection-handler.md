# Module 9: Objection Handler

**Purpose:** Surface the real objections a buyer will have and answer them directly — both woven into the page copy and in a dedicated FAQ. Unaddressed objections are silent page-killers; the reader doesn't tell you they had one, they just leave.

## Process

1. **Start from Module 3's objection list**, then add any category-specific objections that list might have missed: price ("is this worth it?"), time ("I don't have time for this"), fit ("will this work for someone like me / at my stage?"), trust ("why should I believe this?"), and past-failure ("I've tried things like this before and it didn't work").
2. **Write a direct rebuttal for each** — acknowledge it's a real concern before answering it; dismissive answers read as defensive.
3. **Decide placement**: the 1-2 strongest objections should be handled in the page body itself (near where they'd naturally arise — e.g. price objection near the pricing section), the rest go in a FAQ.
4. **Write the FAQ in the customer's actual phrasing** (from Module 3's phrase bank), as a real question, not a marketing-friendly rewrite of it.
5. **Order the FAQ to the blueprint's six slots.** Block 12 of `00-page-blueprint.md` treats the FAQ as a persuasion sequence, not a support page — the order is the mechanism:

   1. **Their biggest objection, reframed as their biggest opportunity.** The strongest objection goes first, on purpose: leaving it to the bottom means the reader carries it through everything above.
   2. **Position against the competitors or alternatives** — including "do nothing" and "figure it out myself" — and get buy-in on *your* way of doing it.
   3. **An objection that springboards into the fuller unique selling proposition.** This is the slot where the answer is allowed to be longer than the question.
   4. **Offer mechanics** — bonuses, drip schedule, how access works, how long they keep it.
   5. **The guarantee / refund policy**, answered with Module 10's guarantee copy itself rather than a paraphrase.
   6. **"Can you remind me of everything I get when I sign up today?"** — answered with the value stack, which hands straight off into the next CTA block.

   Extra questions go **after** slot 6, never in the middle — slot 6 is a transition and anything following it breaks the handoff. If a slot has no genuine question for this offer, say so rather than padding it.
6. **Also write the headline for the section.** "Frequently Asked Questions" is the weakest option available. Prefer the buyer-framed variants: "Questions our top students asked before enrolling in [offer]" / "What people asked before signing up for [offer]" / "What our most successful clients asked before joining". Only use one if it is true.

## Output format

```
OBJECTIONS HANDLED IN-BODY:
1. [Objection] → [Rebuttal] → placement: [block #]
2. ...

FAQ SECTION HEADLINE: [buyer-framed variant]

FAQ (blueprint slot order):
1. [biggest objection → biggest opportunity]
   Q: [customer-phrased question]
   A: [direct, honest answer]
2. [position vs alternatives]
   Q: / A:
3. [objection → fuller USP]
   Q: / A:
4. [offer mechanics: bonuses, access, schedule]
   Q: / A:
5. [guarantee / refunds — answered with Module 10 copy]
   Q: / A:
6. [remind me what I get → hands off to CTA]
   Q: / A:
ADDITIONAL (after slot 6): [any extras]
```

## Handoff

The in-body objections get woven into Module 13's assembly at the noted placement, not bolted on as an afterthought. The FAQ becomes block 12 in blueprint order, and slot 6's answer must lead directly into CTA block #3 — Module 13 checks that join.
