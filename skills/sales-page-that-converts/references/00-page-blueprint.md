# Module 0: The Page Blueprint

**Purpose:** This is the shape of the page every other module is building toward. Read it before Module 4 (Architect) and again before Module 13 (Assembly). The pipeline decides *what* the page says; this blueprint decides *what order it says it in and how many times it asks for the sale*.

It is modelled on the Amy Porterfield long-form launch page — a structure with a long commercial track record for high-ticket courses, programmes and productised services. Treat it as the default. Deviating is allowed but must be a decision with a stated reason (see **Deviation rules**), not a drift.

## The one-line summary

Promise → qualify the reader → show the end state → prove it → **ask** → show the work → stack the value → **ask** → remove the risk → answer the objections → **ask** → let them self-select → speak human to human → **ask**.

The asks are the structure. A page with one CTA at the bottom is not this page.

## The block order

| # | Block | Its job | Fed by |
|---|-------|---------|--------|
| 0 | Header bar | Logo left, primary CTA button right. Visible before the reader scrolls. | 12 |
| 1 | Hero headline stack | Three stacked lines: the biggest desirable **outcome** / the **timeframe** / **without** pain 1, pain 2, pain 3. | 5 |
| 2 | Qualifier + head-nod | Name who this is for, then agree with them. Ends by telling them they are in the right place and to keep reading. Carries an image. | 6, 3 |
| 3 | End-state promise | "By the end of [the programme], you will have…" — **five** specific outcomes, each with why it matters and what it looks like. | 2, 3 |
| 4 | Single testimonial | One proof block, alone, so it is actually read. | 8 |
| 5 | **CTA block #1** | The price/CTA block, in full. First ask. | 11, 12 |
| 6 | Testimonial cluster | One to three more proof blocks. | 8 |
| 7 | What's Inside | Per module: title, a benefit-driven description of the mini-transformation, then "Module Highlights:" + 3 bullets. | 7 |
| 8 | Enrolment recap | "When you enrol during this period, you'll get:" — the offer with its value, the module count, a results recap, then the specific wins, each answering *what does this make possible?* | 7 |
| 9 | Bonuses | "Plus These Bonuses to Help You [benefit]" — per bonus: title, 1–2 sentence result promise, its value, "What You'll Get:" + 3 bullets. | 7 |
| 10 | Value stack → price | Every line item with its value → **Total Value: $X** → "But when you enrol today, you'll get access to everything for just:" → **CTA block #2**. | 7, 11 |
| 11 | Guarantee | Named guarantee + badge. Length, the actions they will take in that window, the tangible *and* emotional benefits they should see, and the **exact** refund process. | 10 |

| 15 | Founder letter | Photo, "I can't wait for you to join [offer]", 1–2 paragraphs of *why you built this*, who it is for, a reminder that it is risk-free, one final invitation, signature. | 12 |

## The CTA block is one block, reused

Blocks 5, 10, 13 and 16 are **the same block**, not four different sections. Build it once:

```
Enrol in [Offer] Today

[X] MONTHLY PAYMENTS OF        OR        A ONE-TIME PAYMENT OF
       $[XX]                                     $[XX]
[Enrol + Pay Monthly]                    [Pay in Full + Save]

[payment method logos]
```

Rules:
- **Two payment routes side by side**, with the pay-in-full route carrying the saving. This is the pattern; do not reduce it to one button unless the offer genuinely has one payment route.
- **Button copy states the action and the route**, not "Buy Now". Vary the *surrounding line* between placements (Module 12), not the block.
- **Payment logos go under the buttons** — they carry a real trust lift at the moment of decision.
- If the offer is quote-based or sales-call-led, the block becomes one button ("Book a call") plus the two-route line dropped. Keep everything else.

## The FAQ is ordered

The FAQ is a persuasion sequence, not a support page. Six slots, in this order:

1. **Their biggest objection, reframed as their biggest opportunity.**
2. **Position against the competitors or alternatives** — and get buy-in on *your* way of doing it.
3. **An objection that springboards into the fuller unique selling proposition.**
4. **Offer mechanics** — bonuses, drip schedule, access, how long they have it.
5. **The guarantee / refund policy** — answered with the guarantee copy itself.
6. **"Can you remind me of everything I get?"** — which transitions straight into CTA block #3.

Extra questions go after slot 6, never in the middle. Every question stays in the buyer's own phrasing from Module 3.

## Translating it off "course"

The source template says *enrol* and *module*. Do not ship that vocabulary to a client selling something else. Translate the whole page consistently:

| Blueprint word | Course | Service / retainer | SaaS | Coaching / consulting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enrol | Enrol | Get started / Book a call | Start free trial | Apply |
| Module | Module | Phase / workstream | Feature / workflow | Session |
| What's Inside | What's Inside | How We Work | What You Get | What We Cover |
| "By the end of this programme" | as-is | "By the end of your first 90 days" | "Within your first month" | "By the end of our work together" |
| Bonuses | Bonuses | Included at no extra cost | Included in every plan | Extras |
| Students | Students | Clients | Customers / teams | Clients |

Pick the column in Module 1 and hold it for the whole page. A page that says "enrol" in the hero and "book a call" in the footer reads as a template someone forgot to finish.

## Deviation rules

The blueprint assumes a **problem-aware to solution-aware** buyer and a considered price. Module 3 gives the awareness stage; Module 4 decides the deviation.

- **Most-aware / warm list / repeat buyer** → collapse to blocks 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 11, 16. Over-explaining to a warm buyer costs conversions.
- **Low ticket / impulse price** → drop blocks 8, 14, 15 and one CTA block. The value stack is doing work the price does not need.
- **Unaware buyer** → the blueprint is not enough on its own. Add a problem/agitation section between blocks 2 and 3, per Module 4.

**Never drop, at any awareness stage:** the hero headline stack (1), the end-state promise (3), at least one proof block (4 or 6), what's inside (7), the value stack and price (10), the guarantee (11), the FAQ (12), and **at least two** CTA blocks.

## Honesty guardrails

The blueprint's persuasion pressure makes it easy to write things that are not true. These are hard lines, and they override the structure:

- **Component values must be defensible** — what the thing would actually cost or sell for on its own. A stacked total nobody believes costs more trust than the anchoring gains.
- **"During this special, limited time period" needs a real deadline.** No cohort close, no price rise, no capacity cap → rewrite the line without the urgency. Module 12 owns this.
- **The guarantee's refund process must be the real one**, including anything the buyer has to do. A guarantee with an undisclosed condition is the single fastest way to turn a customer into a complaint.
- **Testimonials are edited, never authored.** Module 8's rules apply. Placeholder proof in blocks 4 and 6 is flagged to the user as a launch blocker, not quietly filled with plausible-sounding quotes.
