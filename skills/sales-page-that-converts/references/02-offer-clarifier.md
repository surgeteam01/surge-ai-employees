# Module 2: Offer Clarifier

**Purpose:** Sharpen the offer and the transformation it delivers before a word of page copy is written. Most underperforming sales pages fail here, not at the headline — the offer itself is vague.

## Process

1. **State the current offer in one sentence**, as the user described it.
2. **Stress-test it against the Value Equation**: Dream Outcome (how big/specific is the result?), Likelihood of Success (how believable is it?), Time Delay (how fast do they get there?), Effort & Sacrifice (how easy is it?). Flag whichever of these four is weakest or missing.
3. **Rewrite the offer as a before → after transformation**, not a feature list. Bad: "12 weekly coaching calls." Good: "Go from guessing your pricing to closing at your target rate within 6 weeks, with a call to fall back on any time you freeze up on a client."
4. **Check specificity.** Vague promises ("grow your business," "feel more confident") get rejected by readers before they even engage. Push for numbers, timeframes, and named outcomes wherever honestly possible.
5. **Flag anything unprovable or overclaimed** — don't let an inflated promise go forward; it will surface again at Module 9 (Objection Handler) as a credibility problem, or worse, as a compliance problem.

## Output format

```
CURRENT OFFER (as given): [one sentence]
WEAKEST LINK: [Dream Outcome / Likelihood / Time / Effort]
SHARPENED OFFER (one sentence): [rewrite]
TRANSFORMATION STATEMENT (one paragraph, before → after): [rewrite]
RED FLAGS: [anything overclaimed, vague, or unprovable]
```

## Handoff

The sharpened offer and transformation statement become the backbone of Module 4 (Architect), Module 5 (Headline Lab), and Module 6 (Lead Writer). If the user pushes back on the sharpened version, don't just default back to their original wording — ask which specific part felt wrong and refine, since a vague offer will weaken every downstream module.
