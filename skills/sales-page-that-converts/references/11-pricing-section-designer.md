# Module 11: Pricing Section Designer

**Purpose:** Design the pricing section itself — anchoring, justification, tiers, and payment options — so the price feels justified rather than arbitrary.

## Process

1. **Anchor before revealing the price**, using the value stack from Module 7 (total value vs. actual price) so the price lands as a discount against value, not a cost in isolation.
2. **Decide tier structure**, if any: single option (simplest, best when the offer is singular), 2-3 tiers (good/better/best — the middle tier is usually the one designed to be most attractive), or a single option with a payment-plan alternative.
3. **Justify the price** in one line tied back to the transformation (Module 2), not just the deliverables — buyers pay for outcomes, not features.
4. **Present payment options clearly** (one-time vs. installments) if applicable, and note any early-bird/cohort pricing honestly — only include urgency here if it's real (see Module 12 for urgency ethics).
5. **Build the blueprint's CTA block, once.** `00-page-blueprint.md` uses a single price/CTA block reused at blocks 5, 10, 13 and 16. Its default shape is **two payment routes side by side**:

   ```
   Enrol in [Offer] Today

   [X] MONTHLY PAYMENTS OF        OR        A ONE-TIME PAYMENT OF
          $[XX]                                     $[XX]
   [Enrol + Pay Monthly]                    [Pay in Full + Save]

   [payment method logos]
   ```

   - The pay-in-full route **carries the saving**, and the button says so. Two routes give the reader a choice between two ways of buying rather than a choice between buying and not buying, and the monthly figure lets a considered price clear an affordability objection without discounting.
   - **Payment logos sit under the buttons.** They are a real trust lift at the exact moment of decision, and they cost nothing.
   - **Reduce to one button only when the offer genuinely has one route.** If it is quote-based or sales-call-led, the block becomes a single button ("Book a call") with the two-route line dropped — everything else stays.
   - Build it **once**. Module 12 varies the line *above* the block per placement; the block itself does not change down the page, because a reader who scrolled past it twice should recognise it instantly the third time.
6. **Say the price out loud in the "But…" line.** The blueprint's reveal is "But when you enrol today, you'll get access to everything for just:" directly under **Total Value: $X**. The contrast is the whole mechanism, so the two numbers stay adjacent — never separated by a testimonial or an image.

## Output format

```
PRICING STRUCTURE: [single / tiered / payment-plan]
ANCHOR LINE: [value stack recap → Total Value → "But when you enrol today…"]
PRICE PRESENTATION:
[Tier name] — $[price] — [what's included, one line]
(repeat per tier if applicable)
JUSTIFICATION LINE: [ties price to transformation]

CTA BLOCK (built once, reused at blocks 5, 10, 13, 16):
  Heading: [Enrol in [Offer] Today]
  Route A: [X] monthly payments of $[XX] — button: [...]
  Route B: one-time payment of $[XX] — button: [...] (carries the saving)
  Payment logos: [yes / n/a]
  Single-route instead? [no / yes — reason]
```

## Handoff

This block is placed four times by Module 13 in blueprint order. Module 12 owns the line above each placement and the founder letter that precedes the last one — the pricing block and the CTA should read as one continuous push, not two separate sections.
