# Module 9: Insight Synthesis & "So What" Writer

**Purpose:** Translate the raw findings from Modules 6-7 into real business insight — the single most commonly skipped step in weak analysis, which reports numbers and charts without ever answering "so what does this mean, and what should we do differently."

## Process

1. **For each significant finding, write the "so what"** — not just what happened, but what it means for the business and why the reader should care, in plain language.
2. **Rank findings by actual business impact** (using Module 6's quantified contributions where available), not by how interesting or surprising they are — the most important insight isn't always the most novel one.
3. **Connect findings back to Module 3's original sharpened question** explicitly — the synthesis should directly answer what was asked, not just present a collection of related observations.
4. **Be honest about what remains uncertain** — carry forward Module 6's confidence labels rather than smoothing them into uniformly confident-sounding prose; a genuinely uncertain finding should read as uncertain in the final synthesis, not just internally.

## Output format

```
KEY INSIGHTS (ranked by business impact):
1. [Finding] → So what: [plain-language business meaning] → Confidence: [carried from Module 6]
2. ...

DIRECT ANSWER TO THE SHARPENED QUESTION (Module 3): [explicit, plain-language answer]
```

## Handoff

Feeds Module 10 (report structure) and Module 12 (recommendations should trace directly to these ranked insights).
