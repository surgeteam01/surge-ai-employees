# Module 10: Sales Sequence Writer

**Purpose:** Write the full pitch sequence — the emails that actually ask for the sale (or booked call, or trial signup). This is where the offer stack, proof, guarantee, objections, and pricing from earlier modules come together as a coordinated multi-email push, rather than one dense sales page compressed into a single email.

## Process

1. **Confirm sequence length from Module 4's map.** A typical Sales sequence runs 4-6 emails; use fewer for most-aware/warm lists and the full range for colder lists that need more persuasion runway.
2. **Email 1 — Open the offer.** Announce the offer plainly, referencing the soft transition tease from the Welcome sequence or the bridge from the Nurture sequence rather than starting cold. Present the offer stack (every component named and valued, the same value-anchoring logic used in a sales page's offer stack: list every component, assign an honest perceived value, order by weight, and let the gap between total value and actual price do the persuading).
3. **Email 2 — Proof.** Lead with the strongest proof piece from Module 8, matched to the specific outcome this offer delivers. Keep the pitch present but secondary to the proof itself.
4. **Email 3 — Guarantee.** Write an ethical, confidence-building guarantee that reduces the buyer's perceived risk without over-promising or creating a claim the business can't honor. Confirm what the business can actually stand behind before drafting — never write a guarantee stronger than what the user has confirmed they'll honor. Choose a guarantee type that fits the offer: unconditional (highest trust, best when confident in fit), conditional (refund tied to the buyer doing X and not getting Y), performance-based (tied to a measurable outcome, only if genuinely reliable), or extended/double (e.g. "keep the bonuses even if you refund").
5. **Objection email(s)** — insert Module 9's dedicated objection email(s) at this point in the sequence, addressing the 1-2 strongest objections in full.
6. **Email — Pricing and payment options.** Anchor the price against the value stack from step 2 so it lands as a discount against value, not a cost in isolation. Present tier structure if any (single option, or 2-3 tiers with the middle tier usually designed to be most attractive), payment plan options, and one justification line tying the price back to the transformation (Module 2), not just the deliverables — buyers pay for outcomes, not features.
7. **Keep every email focused on one job.** Resist stacking proof + guarantee + pricing into a single email just to save an email — the sequence's power comes from each email getting full attention for one persuasive element, the same way a sales page dedicates a full section to each rather than cramming.

## Output format

```
SALES SEQUENCE ([count] emails):

EMAIL 1 — Open the Offer
Body: [full email copy, including offer stack]
OFFER STACK RECAP:
- [Component] — Value: $[x] — [one line]
(repeat)
TOTAL VALUE: $[sum] | ACTUAL PRICE: $[price]

EMAIL 2 — Proof
Body: [full email copy, built around strongest proof piece]

EMAIL 3 — Guarantee
GUARANTEE TYPE: [chosen type] — reason: [why this fits]
Body: [full email copy]

OBJECTION EMAIL(S) — [from Module 9, inserted here]

PRICING EMAIL
PRICING STRUCTURE: [single / tiered / payment-plan]
Body: [full email copy — anchor, price reveal, justification line, payment options]
```

## Handoff

This full sequence is the persuasive core of the funnel — Module 11 (Cart-Close) picks up immediately where this sequence's last email leaves off, so the final Sales email should end in a way that makes sense as a lead-in to urgency, not as a dead end. Module 12 supplies the actual CTA copy/buttons used throughout; this module focuses on the persuasive content, not the click mechanics.
