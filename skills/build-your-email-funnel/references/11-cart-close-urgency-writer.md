# Module 11: Cart-Close / Urgency Sequence Writer

**Purpose:** Write the final push sequence — the emails that run as a real deadline (cart close, cohort start, bonus expiry, price increase) approaches. This sequence converts the readers who were persuaded by the Sales sequence but hadn't yet acted; its entire job is giving them a real reason to act now.

## Process

1. **Confirm a real deadline exists before writing a word of this sequence.** Only use urgency/scarcity that is real — a genuine cart-close date, real limited capacity, an actual price increase date, a bonus that truly expires. Never fabricate false scarcity (a recurring "cart closes tonight" that reopens next week, a fake countdown, "only 3 spots left" when untrue). This is both an ethics line and, practically, something that destroys list trust and tanks every future sequence once a reader catches it once. If no real deadline exists, say so plainly and recommend either creating one honestly (e.g. an actual cohort start date, an actual bonus with a real cutoff) or skipping this sequence entirely.
2. **Confirm sequence length and cadence from Module 4's map.** Cart-close sequences compress in time as the deadline nears — typically 3-4 emails over the final 24-72 hours, with cadence tightening (e.g. 48 hours out, 24 hours out, morning-of, final hours).
3. **Email 1 (deadline announced) — Name the deadline plainly** and restate the core transformation in one or two lines; this reader has already seen the full pitch, so this email's job is reminding, not re-persuading from scratch.
4. **Email 2 (mid-countdown) — Answer the single most common last-minute hesitation** (often price or "not sure it's the right time") in a few sentences, referencing Module 9's objection work if a matching one exists; don't repeat the full objection email verbatim, just its core line.
5. **Final email(s) (last chance) — Short, direct, and honest about the stakes**: what happens if they don't act (the door closes, the price goes up, the bonus disappears) stated factually, not manipulatively. This is the one place in the funnel where brevity itself is persuasive — a long final-hours email reads as anxious, not confident.
6. **Never manufacture guilt or shame** ("last chance to finally fix your life") — urgency should be about the real deadline, not an attack on the reader for not having acted yet.

## Output format

```
CART-CLOSE SEQUENCE ([count] emails, cadence: [spacing before deadline]):
REAL DEADLINE: [what it is, and why it's real] — or "none available: recommend not fabricating one"

EMAIL 1 — Deadline Announced
Body: [full email copy]

EMAIL 2 — Mid-Countdown / Objection Reminder
Body: [full email copy]

FINAL EMAIL(S) — Last Chance
Body: [full email copy, stating stakes plainly]
```

## Handoff

Feeds directly into Module 13 for placement as the funnel's final sequence, and Module 14 will check whether the urgency used here is both honest and strong enough, flagging it if the sequence was skipped for lack of a real deadline (a funnel that ends without a cart-close sequence should say so explicitly, not silently be missing one).
