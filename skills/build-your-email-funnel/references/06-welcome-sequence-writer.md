# Module 6: Welcome Sequence Writer

**Purpose:** Write the sequence that runs the moment someone joins the list — deliver on the immediate promise, set expectations for what's coming, and build enough trust that the reader opens the next email. This is the email-funnel equivalent of a sales page lead: it's where the reader decides whether this sender is worth their inbox space.

## Process

1. **Confirm sequence length from Module 4's map.** A typical Welcome sequence is 2-4 emails; use the awareness-stage and list-warmth guidance from that module rather than defaulting to a fixed number.
2. **Email 1 — Deliver and set expectations.** Give the reader whatever they signed up for (the lead magnet, the resource, the discount code) immediately and without friction — never bury the thing they asked for behind more asks. Then set expectations: what will they get from this list, how often, and what's coming next. End with one small, easy action (reply to the email, follow a link to the best piece of content) rather than a pitch.
3. **Email 2 — Build authority/connection.** Tell the origin story or the credibility case for why this sender/business is worth listening to — concretely, not as a generic "About Us." Use a story or scene, not a resume. This is the email-equivalent of a sales page lead's hook: pick a style that matches awareness stage and voice:
   - **Story/scene opener** — drop the reader into a specific, relatable moment (avoid generic "Have you ever felt..." openers; be concrete).
   - **Direct address** — speak straight to the reader's specific situation, useful for warmer/most-aware lists who don't need a long trust-building arc.
   - Use the exact-phrase bank from Module 3 throughout — this is where mirroring the customer's own words matters most, since it's early proof the sender "gets" them.
4. **Email 3 (if in scope) — Bridge to value.** Transition from "who we are" to "what we know" — tease the kind of value the Nurture sequence (Module 7) is about to deliver, so the sequence handoff feels planned rather than abrupt.
5. **Final Welcome email — Soft transition tease.** If the funnel goal (Module 1) is a direct sale, plant a single line hinting that there's a specific offer coming later in the relationship — don't pitch yet, just remove the surprise when the Sales sequence eventually arrives.
6. **Keep every email short.** Welcome emails are read on a phone in a few seconds; favor short paragraphs and one idea per email over a comprehensive one.

## Output format

```
WELCOME SEQUENCE ([count] emails):

EMAIL 1 — Deliver & Set Expectations
Body: [full email copy]
CTA: [the one small action]

EMAIL 2 — Authority/Connection
Style: [story/scene or direct address]
Body: [full email copy]
CTA: [the one small action]

EMAIL 3 — Bridge to Value (if in scope)
Body: [full email copy]

FINAL WELCOME EMAIL — Soft Transition Tease (if funnel goal is a direct sale)
Body: [full email copy, ending with the planted line about what's coming]
```

## Handoff

Whichever voice and pacing this sequence establishes should carry forward into Module 7 (Nurture) — Module 13 (Assembler) should treat a tone shift between Welcome and Nurture as a defect to fix, not a stylistic choice. The soft transition tease (if written) is a direct handoff to Module 10 (Sales Sequence), which should open by referencing it rather than starting cold.
