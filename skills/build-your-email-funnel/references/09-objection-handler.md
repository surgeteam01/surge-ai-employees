# Module 9: Objection Handler

**Purpose:** Surface the real objections a buyer will have and answer them directly — some woven into Sales sequence emails, others given a full dedicated objection email. Unaddressed objections are silent funnel-killers; the reader doesn't reply to tell you they had one, they just stop opening.

## Process

1. **Start from Module 3's objection list**, then add any category-specific objections that list might have missed: price ("is this worth it?"), time ("I don't have time for this"), fit ("will this work for someone like me / at my stage?"), trust ("why should I believe this, from an email?"), and past-failure ("I've tried things like this before and it didn't work").
2. **Write a direct rebuttal for each** — acknowledge it's a real concern before answering it; dismissive answers read as defensive, and in an email that's an easy delete.
3. **Decide placement**: the 1-2 strongest objections should get a dedicated objection email of their own in the Sales sequence (an email whose entire subject and body is built around answering one real doubt, often framed as an FAQ or as "you might be thinking..."), since email affords room a page's FAQ section doesn't. The rest can be woven as a P.S. line or a short paragraph in other Sales sequence emails.
4. **Write the dedicated objection email in the customer's actual phrasing** (from Module 3's phrase bank) — open with the objection as the reader would actually think it, not a marketing-friendly rewrite of it.

## Output format

```
DEDICATED OBJECTION EMAIL(S):
Subject/theme: [the objection, in customer phrasing]
Body: [full email copy — acknowledge, then rebut]

OBJECTIONS WOVEN INTO OTHER EMAILS:
1. [Objection] → [Rebuttal] → placement: [which Sales sequence email, e.g. "Sales #3, as a P.S."]
2. ...
```

## Handoff

The dedicated objection email(s) become their own numbered email(s) in Module 10's Sales sequence at the position noted. The woven objections get inserted into Module 10's other emails at assembly time (Module 13) rather than bolted on as an afterthought.
