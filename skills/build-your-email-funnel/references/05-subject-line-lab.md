# Module 5: Subject Line Lab

**Purpose:** Write and score subject lines and preview text for every email in the funnel. The subject line is the single highest-leverage line in an email funnel — nothing else it contains matters if the email doesn't get opened.

## Process

1. **Generate at least 4-6 subject line options per email** (fewer than a sales page headline, since this repeats per email across the whole funnel), drawing on the sharpened offer (Module 2), the top desire/pain (Module 3), and multiple formulas so options are genuinely different, not just re-worded:
   - **Curiosity gap**: opens a question the reader wants closed, without being clickbait relative to what's inside — e.g. "the pricing mistake that's costing you clients."
   - **Direct benefit**: states the payoff plainly — e.g. "how to close at your target rate."
   - **Customer-language mirror**: built directly from the exact-phrase bank in Module 3.
   - **Personal/plain-text style**: reads like a one-to-one email, not a broadcast — e.g. "quick question" or "this didn't work for me either" — effective for welcome and nurture sequences, riskier for sales sequences where clarity about the pitch matters more.
   - **Urgency/deadline** (Sales and Cart-Close sequences only): only when the urgency is real — see Module 11.
2. **Write matching preview text (the preheader)** for each subject line — this is the email-specific equivalent of a subhead. It should extend the subject line's promise or add the specificity the subject line didn't have room for, never just repeat the subject line verbatim.
3. **Score each option** on: Open-worthiness (does it earn a click in the inbox in under 2 seconds?), Clarity (is it obvious what the email is about, or is the curiosity gap so wide it feels like bait?), Relevance (does it speak to the actual audience segment and where they are in the sequence?), and Deliverability safety (does it avoid spam-trigger words and patterns — ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation!!!, "free," "guarantee," "act now," dollar signs in the subject line itself — that hurt inbox placement regardless of how good the copy is?).
4. **Pick the top option per email** and note why it wins.

## Output format

```
EMAIL: [sequence name + position, e.g. "Welcome #2"]
SUBJECT LINE OPTIONS (scored 1-5 on Open-worthiness / Clarity / Relevance / Deliverability):
1. [subject] — O:_ C:_ R:_ D:_
2. ...

TOP PICK: [subject line]
PREVIEW TEXT: [preheader line]
Why this wins: [one or two sentences]
```

## Handoff

The top pick's language should echo forward into that email's body (Module 6, 7, 10, or 11, depending on sequence) so the transition from subject line to opening line feels seamless, not like two different writers. Deliverability flags from this module should also inform Module 14's final deliverability check — if multiple subject lines across the funnel lean on the same risky pattern, that's a funnel-wide issue, not a one-email fix.
