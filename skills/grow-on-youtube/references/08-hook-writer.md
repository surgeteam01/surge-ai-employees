# Module 8: Hook Writer

**Purpose:** Write the actual first 15-30 seconds (or 1-3 seconds for Shorts) — adapted from `sales-page-that-converts`'s Lead Writer logic, since the underlying styles (PAS, story/scene opener, bold claim + proof tease, direct address) apply directly, but the stakes are higher and the window shorter: YouTube's own retention graphs show the steepest drop-off in the opening seconds of almost any video.

## Process

1. **Pick a hook style matched to the video's premise** (Module 5/6): a bold-claim + proof tease hook works well when the packaging promised a specific result; a PAS (problem-agitate) hook works well for problem-aware audiences; a story/scene opener works well when the content is personal or narrative-driven; a direct address ("if you're someone who...") works well for a highly specific, self-selecting audience.
2. **Never restate the title verbatim as the opening line** — the hook's job is to add something the title/thumbnail didn't already say, confirming the viewer made the right choice to click rather than just repeating the premise back at them.
3. **For long-form, get to a concrete promise or preview within the first 15-30 seconds** — avoid slow, generic channel intros or lengthy pleasantries before the actual value starts; viewers decide to stay or leave almost immediately.
4. **For Shorts, compress to 1-3 seconds** — often just a single strong visual/verbal statement with no room for a traditional multi-beat hook structure.
5. **Use the exact-phrase bank from Module 3** where it fits naturally.

## Output format

```
VIDEO: [from Module 5/6]
FORMAT: [long-form / Shorts]
HOOK STYLE: [PAS / story / bold-claim / direct-address]
HOOK SCRIPT: [exact opening lines, timed to the format's window]
```
