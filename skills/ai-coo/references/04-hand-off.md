# Module 4: Hand-off

**Purpose:** Once the route is clear, move the founder forward — into the skill if it is here, and to the right employee if it is not. Describing what a skill would do and then stopping is a dead end; so is quietly doing the specialist's job yourself.

## When the skill is installed

1. Say the route in one line: the skill, why, and what it will need from them (Module 1's tables say what each produces; the skill's own opening stage collects the rest).
2. **Carry the brain forward.** If `BUSINESS-BRAIN.md` exists, tell the skill it is there — it reads the file at the start of its onboarding and skips what the file answers. If the founder said anything in this conversation the brain does not hold (a new price, a new goal), pass that along explicitly.
3. Invoke the skill by its exact name. Do not re-collect what the founder already told you.

## When the skill is NOT installed

This is the case that matters most, and the one to get exactly right.

1. **Say which employee holds it**, by name, from Module 1: "That is `sales-page-that-converts`, which lives in The Growth & Sales Rep — not in the pack you have installed."
2. **Say what it would produce and what it needs**, in one line each, so the founder can judge whether it is worth hiring for.
3. **Say what they can do meanwhile with what they have**, if anything genuinely helps — the Strategist's `nail-your-pricing` before a sales page is real preparation. Do not stretch: if nothing here helps, say so.
4. **Stop.** Do not improvise the missing skill. A sales page written by the COO from general knowledge is worse than no sales page, because it looks like the real thing and is not built on the method. The honest answer and the pointer to the right employee are the same sentence, and that is the only kind of pointer worth giving.

## For a journey that crosses skills

1. State the whole journey up front — every step, its employee tag, one line on why — so the founder sees the plan before the first step runs.
2. Run one skill at a time; finish its pipeline before the next.
3. Carry outputs forward explicitly: a positioning statement into every later onboarding; a voice profile into everything written; a price decided once and referenced, never re-derived.
4. Pause between steps. A finding partway (a narrower audience than expected) can change the rest of the route.
5. If a later step lives in an employee they do not have, say so at the start — not when they arrive at it.
6. If they only want the first step now, run it and name the natural next one.

## What "hand off" means mechanically

Invoke the target skill by its exact name from Module 1, in the way this host invokes a skill. One at a time, never several at once. Then get out of the way: the specialist runs its own pipeline, and you are not part of it until the founder comes back with a new goal.

## Handoff

None beyond the one above. The next time the founder brings an outcome, start again at `SKILL.md`.
